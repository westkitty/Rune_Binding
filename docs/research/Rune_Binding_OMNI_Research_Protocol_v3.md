10. Unresolved claims, missing witnesses, scholarly disagreements, and genuinely unknown origins.

# 1. Evidence Ontology: Six Things That Must Never Be Confused

For every rune, symbol, diagram, and claim, record these separately:

| Dimension | What is being claimed? | What would substantiate it? |
|---|---|---|
| **Graphic form** | These exact strokes occurred in this arrangement. | Object photo, rubbing, manuscript facsimile, dated printed source, or image witness. |
| **Textual reading** | The marks represent these characters or words. | Transcription and scholarly epigraphic reading, with alternatives. |
| **Linguistic meaning** | The words or rune names mean X. | Philology, dictionaries, critical texts, etymology. |
| **Practical function** | The design served a specific purpose. | Accompanying instruction, meaningful context, genre, or defensible inference. |
| **Date and origin** | It belongs to a period/tradition. | Dated artifact or manuscript witness; distinguish date of copying from date of invention. |
| **Continuity / genealogy** | Modern design B descends from design A. | Documented transmission, named intermediary, dated independent witnesses, not resemblance alone. |

Never use a single "authentic / fake" judgment to replace these questions. A symbol can have historical letterforms, a modern composition, and a genuinely meaningful modern function at the same time.

## Evidence states

- **ATTESTED:** The specified feature or claim is demonstrated by an identifiable surviving witness.
- **SCHOLARLY RECONSTRUCTION:** A reconstruction supported by named philological or historical argument.
- **PLAUSIBLE HYPOTHESIS:** Compatible with evidence but unproven.
- **LATER HISTORICAL TRADITION:** Securely documented, but from a later period than a popular claim assigns.
- **MODERN DOCUMENTED:** Secure evidence of a recent construction, reading, or use.
- **UNVERIFIED:** No sufficient source located in the research actually performed.
- **CONTRADICTED / MISATTRIBUTED:** A specific dated or identity claim conflicts with stronger evidence.

**Negative evidence rule:** "Not found in the searched databases and scholarship" is not "never existed." State searches performed, their coverage, and the limits of the surviving corpus. Distinguish a true contradiction from a mere absence of witnesses.

## Provenance scale — retained from original brief, with a correction

Apply `P5` through `P0` **to an explicitly stated historical provenance claim**, never indiscriminately to an image's spiritual value.

- **P5:** Exact design and asserted date/function directly supported by an identified primary witness.
- **P4:** Strongly corroborated scholarly reconstruction of the specified attribute; original not fully available.
- **P3:** Historical graphic element attested but claimed purpose, chronology, or composition substantially inferred.
- **P2:** Secure later-period historical witness for the actual symbol/use, but earlier origin unproven.
- **P1:** Demonstrably modern rune-inspired construction or meaning, honestly so labeled.
- **P0:** Specific popular attribution demonstrably contradicted by reliable evidence or an identified fabrication.
- **PU:** Unresolved: inadequate evidence to score. **Do not automatically give P0 to an unknown origin.**

**MANDATORY:** Also report `FORM`, `FUNCTION`, `DATE`, and `TRANSMISSION` independently as **documented / reconstructed / disputed / not established**. The provenance scale is a display aid, not a substitute for analysis. For example, a well-attested 1860 magical stave can have excellent manuscript provenance despite not being Viking Age.

Confidence = `HIGH`, `MEDIUM`, `LOW`, or `UNRESOLVED`, with a one-sentence rationale; do not invent numeric probabilities.

# 2. Primary Sources, Independence, and Citation Rules

## Preferred source order

1. Archaeological inscription or manuscript witness: catalogue ID, collection/shelfmark, date estimate, photo/facsimile, context.
2. Scholarly critical editions, peer-reviewed studies, monographs, archaeological reports, established runological corpora.
3. Specialist institutions and curated databases with explicit provenance and editorial notes.
4. Modern practitioners and occult authors, **for documenting the origin and reception of modern claims**, not as retrospective proof of ancient practice.
5. Social-media posts, merch, Pinterest/Etsy/TikTok/SEO sites, **only as attributable evidence for current circulation or a bounded online provenance trail**.

A manuscript catalogue is evidence that the manuscript exists and how it is catalogued; it does not automatically establish that a depicted custom began on that manuscript's copying date. A medieval literary text is primary evidence of what the surviving text says, not automatic proof of an earlier historical event.

## Verified starting points — research leads, not preapproved verdicts

- Swedish National Heritage Board / Uppsala University, **Runor**: https://runor.raa.se/search
- Uppsala University, **Scandinavian Runic-text Database**: https://www.uu.se/en/department/scandinavian-languages/research/research-projects/scandinavian-runic-text-database
- Göttingen-led **RuneS-DB**: https://runesdb.eu/en/
- University of Copenhagen / National Museum of Denmark, **Danske Runeindskrifter**: https://nors.ku.dk/english/research/runes/
- **Menota Handbook**, ch. 18, runic transcriptions and bind-rune encoding (draft; check status): https://www.menota.org/HB4_ch18.xml
- **Handrit manuscript catalogue** and relevant national repositories; begin with the record for *Huld*, `ÍB 383 4to`, and verify physical/digital witness and folio.
- Mindy MacLeod, *Bind-runes: An Investigation of Ligatures in Runic Epigraphy* (Uppsala, 2002); track page-specific claims and access limits.
- Relevant rune poems, critical editions of *Hávamál*, *Sigrdrífumál*, *Egils saga*, and actual associated manuscript witnesses.

Check the primary source rather than quoting this starter list as proof. Source page migrations, updated readings, and conflicting catalogues must be noted.

## Citation requirements

For every pivotal assertion, supply a resolvable citation: URL/DOI/ISBN **and** where possible entry ID, inventory number, page/folio/line, reading, edition, publication year, and access date. Provide page-specific references for scholarly interpretations. Use original-language quotation only when it is accurate and necessary; present your own short translation with attribution and flag ambiguity.

**Do not invent:** catalogue numbers, exact dates, folios, glyph features, rune sequences, translations, bibliographic pagination, source quotations, earliest appearances, negative searches, or access to restricted works.

**Independence check:** Two websites repeating the same modern author's diagram are one evidence lineage. Two databases syndicating the same archaeological record are potentially one underlying witness. Show shared provenance.

# 3. Data Architecture — Build an Evidence Atlas While Researching

Maintain five linked registers throughout the investigation:

- `SOURCES`: Unique `SRC-###`; full citation, source type, repository, date, stable URL, access status, page/folio, image rights, origin of any interpretation.
- `WITNESSES`: Unique `WIT-###`; physical object or individual manuscript/print/web witness, dating range, place, script, catalogue ID, image references, conservation caveats.
- `DESIGNS`: Unique `DES-###`; exact stroke topology, rune components if justified, orientation, mirrored variants, overlaps, differences between editions, type (`rune`, `ligature`, `same-stave`, `cipher`, `galdrastafur`, `nonrunic`, `uncertain`).
- `CLAIMS`: Unique `CLM-###`; precise assertion, proponent, first known occurrence, best supporting and contradicting witnesses, confidence, verdict, date boundaries, provenance class.
- `TRANSMISSION EDGES`: Unique `EDGE-###`; source design → derived design, copying/adaptation hypothesis, earliest documentation, evidence for **actual transmission** vs mere visual similarity.

Also track `IMG-###` and `ITEM-###` for each supplied image and each individual symbol/label within it. A single screenshot often contains numerous distinct claims; **do not give the entire screenshot one verdict**.

### Philological fidelity / encoding

Keep distinct: `photo/facsimile → diplomatic runic transcription → Latin transliteration → normalized language form → English translation → proposed ritual meaning`. Never jump from a normalized modern word to an alleged original rune shape. Record direction, reversal, uncertainty, damaged characters, orientation, and whether a bind-rune crosses word boundaries. Check scholarly transcription conventions in Menota and the relevant corpus rather than inventing typography.

# 4. Core Historical Investigation

## 4A. Runic systems and the limits of rune "meanings"

Research **Elder Futhark**, **Younger Futhark** (long-branch, short-twig, and staveless variants where relevant), **Anglo-Saxon/Anglo-Frisian Futhorc**, and **Medieval Scandinavian runes**. For each establish:

- Approximate attestation range, distribution, alphabet and sign count, phonetic system, orthographic practices, and later developments.
- Earliest securely dated representative inscriptions and edge cases in scholarly dating.
- Which rune names are attested in manuscripts, which are reconstructed, and which are later normalized labels.
- Rune value vs rune name vs reconstructed proto-form vs literal name sense vs rune-poem imagery vs attested magical function vs modern divinatory keyword.
- Whether inscriptions include the symbol under discussion **in that rune system**, not merely a modern letter-shape analogue.

Use the **Old English Rune Poem**, **Norwegian Rune Poem**, **Icelandic Rune Poem**, and relevant other sources; give manuscript/witness histories, date range, original language, translation, textual variants, and what the poems can and cannot demonstrate about earlier customs.

Test popular concepts individually: `Fehu=wealth/prosperity`, `Gebo=gifts/relationships`, `Algiz=protection`, `Sowilo=victory`, `Berkano=fertility`, `Raidho=travel`, `Isa=stagnation`, `Dagaz=awakening`. Classify each claimed step, not just the final keyword.

## 4B. Genuine bind-runes and their functions

Define a bind-rune using scholarly runology, not contemporary sigil tutorials. Investigate:

- Shared-stave ligatures, same-stave inscriptions, graphic abbreviations, ornamental uses, word/name monograms, possible ritual cases, and uncertain marks.
- Frequency and distribution by **period, region, medium, inscription type**, and corpus coverage. **Avoid timeless generalizations** (especially "rare," "common," or "mostly magical") without denominators.
- Object-by-object examples, catalogue signum, image, inscription reading, actual component runes, date and alternative readings.
- Cases where joined strokes cross word boundaries or readings depend on context; compare scholarly encoding standards.
- Whether the familiar modern process of choosing rune *meanings* then superimposing glyphs is attested before modern occult publications.

Use MacLeod's 2002 monograph where accessible. Independently test useful documented examples such as **N 146**, **N 446**, **B 192**, or **B 493** against the relevant corpus and Menota; do not assert that a specific photographed sign is present until its entry has been checked.

## 4C. Historical runic ritual or magical use

Investigate bracteates, amulets, curses, invocations, healing contexts, protection claims, repeated signs, nonlexical formulae, cryptic writing, divine names, and inscriptions interpreted as ritual. For each, separate **attested inscription text**, **artefact context**, **proposed reading**, and **inferred magical intention**.

Special test cases: `alu`, `laukaz`, `auja`, `gibu auja`, repeated rune sequences, apparent numerical patterning, Óðinn references. For **GIBU AUJA**, identify actual object(s), transcription, scholarly translation, dating, context, and the relationship (if any) between the attested phrase and modern "good luck rune" graphics.

## 4D. Literary runic magic

Inspect primary witnesses and relevant critical editions for *Hávamál*, *Sigrdrífumál*, *Egils saga*, and other defensible cases. Distinguish text composition estimates from extant manuscript date. Record actual words for **victory, ale, birth/helping, sea, branch, speech, thought, and healing** rune categories where attested. For every category ask: **Does the source specify an actual glyph, formula, location of carving, material, operation, or only a named class of practice?** Do not manufacture glyph diagrams where the text gives none.

## 4E. Odin / Óðinn names and modern Odin ligatures

Examine Proto-Norse forms, Viking Age spellings in context, and later Scandinavian manuscript forms. Compare them with the English/Latin-letter sequence `ODIN`. Test the popular stacked `Othala + Dagaz + Isa + Nauthiz` "Odin bind rune": identify the exact earliest witness located, script consistency, implied language, relation to historical inscriptions, and whether a visual analogue actually establishes continuity.

## 4F. Icelandic grimoires and *galdrastafir*

Do **not** merge the early-modern manuscript tradition with Viking Age rune writing. Investigate **Galdrabók**, **Huld (ÍB 383 4to)**, other relevant magical manuscripts, **Ægishjálmur**, **Vegvísir**, **Svefnþorn**, **Lásabrjótur**, **Óttastafur**, and other symbols supplied in the images. Check alternate spellings and which variants are scholarly, manuscript, or modern.

For each: earliest located witness, exact shelfmark, folio/page, date of that witness, relevant Icelandic instruction, original design and variants, proposed function, whether identifiable runic letters are actually present, and later reuses. For **Vegvísir**, explicitly test "Viking compass" against a physical navigational instrument vs a manuscript charm. For **Ægishjálmur**, separate the older literary name/concept from dated surviving drawings. For **Svefnþorn**, investigate historical use rather than importing "restful sleep" wellness terminology.

## 4G. Modern rune-magic genealogy

Trace **antiquarian runology, Romantic nationalism, Guido von List and Armanen runes, völkisch esotericism, Karl Maria Wiligut, interwar and postwar developments, modern Heathenry, Ralph Blum's *The Book of Runes*, Stephen Flowers / Edred Thorsson, modern oracle/divinatory systems, reversal meanings, contemporary sigil magic, tutorials, book charts, and social-media circulation**.

Investigate when and how:

- Each rune acquired a standardized list of psychological/occult keywords.
- Upright/reversed divination acquired its current rules (distinguish actual reversed letterforms from reversed *divinatory meanings*).
- Intention-based overlapping rune graphics emerged and spread.
- Modern designers used rune names, numerology, initials, abstract keywords, and later occult methods in combination.
- Particular internet-era charts became standardized, copied, or mutated.

Do not collapse all modern esotericism or contemporary Heathenry into völkisch ideology. Document specific transmissions, discontinuities, and divergent uses separately. Treat offensive political appropriation historically and specifically, without assigning guilt by graphic resemblance alone.

# 5. Image-by-Image Symbol Forensics

**Required only when original images are actually present and legible.**

### Pass 1 — Inventory

Create one image-level record and an **individual item** record for each legible symbol, caption, label, claimed function, rune name, spelling, and translation. Crop/zoom only through available legitimate tools; if the pixel detail is insufficient, mark `ILLEGIBLE` rather than pretending certainty. Track all repeated icons across screenshots.

### Pass 2 — Graphic identity

Compare actual strokes, shared staves, terminations, joins, intersections, relative angles, scale, line weight, mirroring, rotation, and missing/added elements. Distinguish:

- a single rune in a specific script;
- an attested orthographic bind-rune;
- same-stave or cipher writing;
- a monogram;
- an Icelandic magical stave;
- a modern composite sign;
- a nonrunic or indeterminate drawing.

**False-match defense:** Two designs do not share provenance because they both have eight rays, look like the Algiz rune, or can be decomposed into similar strokes. Where matching depends on editor interpretation, say so.

### Pass 3 — Attribution testing

Find the earliest **exact graphic witness located**, distinguish this from earliest occurrence of **the name**, **the function**, or **any component rune**. Search books, manuscript scans, institutional catalogues, digitized early occult literature, archived web pages, later editions, commercial pages, and social images in a proportionate order. Record dated candidates and archive limitations. Never claim "first ever" merely because a web search located an early page.

### Pass 4 — Claim testing

Check all labels encountered, explicitly including: courage, energy, good luck, grace, health, love, strength, protection, safe travels, prosperity, happy marriage, lasting partnership, eternal love, sexual love, homosexuality / man-to-man love, woman-to-woman love, financial windfall, vehicle protection, academic brilliance, mind power, self-esteem, legal success, home protection, healing, peace, binding, and lock breaking.

For identity- and relationship-related signs, distinguish current descriptive categories and community meaning from any alleged historical identity system. Be exact without dismissing contemporary symbolism.

### Pass 5 — Family resemblance vs proven copying

Build an image/design family tree. Label every edge as `DOCUMENTED COPYING`, `STRONG ATTRIBUTION`, `POSSIBLE INFLUENCE`, `GRAPHIC RESEMBLANCE ONLY`, or `NO KNOWN LINK`. Count shared upstream works once; list alterations and possible transcription mistakes. Retain original-source links and use images under their actual license; **never substitute an AI-generated lookalike for an original manuscript witness**.

### Image item output schema

| Image:item | Claimed name/use | Exact visual description | Type / script | Earliest **located** witness | Date range | Evidence for name | Evidence for purpose | Provenance P0-P5/PU | Confidence | Verdict | Source IDs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Use the user's six original classes when presenting readable visual verdicts: historically attested; historical form with modern interpretation; later historical magic; modern construction; false/misleading attribution; uncertain. These are **presentation categories**, not interchangeable with the underlying evidence fields.

# 7. Research Workflow — Accuracy With Performance Controls

Do the work in **bounded, auditable passes**, preventing a sprawling but shallow result.

### PHASE 0 — Source and asset inventory

1. Enumerate accessible uploaded images; give exact image count and `IMG` IDs.
2. Inventory available catalogues, manuscript repositories, databases, scholarly works, and access restrictions.
3. Build an initial claim backlog from the prompt and image captions.
4. Mark every unavailable input/primary witness as an explicit blocker. **Do not fill gaps by memory.**

**Gate:** Each source and image has a resolvable identity or an honest missing-data state.

### PHASE 1 — Historical baseline

Establish chronology, writing systems, rune poems, and manuscript transmission. Normalize source citations and demonstrate the 6-dimension evidence separation on at least five concrete claims. Resolve contested rune-name reconstructions before trusting downstream meaning tables.

**Gate:** Every major period and writing system has sources; uncertain reconstructions are visible.

### PHASE 2 — Inscription and magical-practice case studies

Investigate the strongest verified examples first: readable bind-runes, ambiguous ligatures, inscriptions plausibly serving ritual functions, and competing scholarly interpretations. Compare at least one ordinary orthographic bind-rune with one proposed magical case before making global claims about purpose.

**Gate:** Actual witness images or credible object descriptions accompany decisive claims; no linguistic interpretation is presented as a direct photo fact.

### PHASE 3 — Manuscript and modern provenance

Trace the Icelandic stave witnesses and modern occult genealogy. Separate `earliest witness located`, `earliest possible exemplar`, and `first documented modern popularity`. Build a directed **evidence graph**, not a forced linear origin story.

**Gate:** Every arrow in the genealogy has cited evidence or an explicit `HYPOTHESIS / GAP` label.

### PHASE 4 — Symbol-forensic pass

Run only when original images are accessible. Inventory every item, then use duplicate detection to investigate repeated signs once and link all instances back to a shared `DES` record. Allocate extra effort to politically charged, commercially widespread, or unusually specific designs only when evidence warrants.

**Gate:** Every legible image item has a record; unresolved items remain unresolved.

### PHASE 5 — Synthesis and adversarial QA

Prepare direct answers, chronology, tables, corrections, source dossier, and honest contemporary design guidance. Revisit strong claims looking for opposing scholarly readings and counterexamples. Run the acceptance tests below.

**Gate:** All strong conclusions are independently traceable; omissions are reported before release.

## Performance rules

- **Evidence first, breadth second.** A thoroughly documented hard example is more useful than dozens of unverified examples.
- **Prioritize claims:** `HIGH` for alleged ancient origin, exact first appearance, identification of a depicted symbol, and links between historical periods; `MEDIUM` for common modern associations; `LOW` for small variant details unless disputed.
- **Search log, not fake comprehensiveness:** Store query, corpus, date, filters, aliases, result, and access state for important *negative* origin claims.
- **Reuse source records:** Do not re-research or re-summarize the same inscription as an independent case every time a website repackages it.
- **Early stop:** When additional searches only repeat the same upstream witness and no longer change a claim's status, stop and record saturation limits.
- **Escalation:** If a key scholarly source is unavailable, rely on a verified secondary quotation only with that limitation made explicit; do not present it as firsthand reading.
- **Chunk long research:** Preserve a short running status with verified claims, open blockers, source IDs, and the next phase, so continuation does not forget evidence or overwrite provisional results.
- **Do not simulate completed research:** A high-quality plan, source bibliography, or invented example is not a completed fact-check.

# 9. Acceptance Tests — Fail Rather Than Fabricate

Before declaring the report finished, check all twenty:

- [ ] **AT-01** Every ancient-origin claim points to an identifiable dated witness or is marked unverified.
- [ ] **AT-02** Distinguishes oldest surviving manuscript from hypothesized older composition or practice.
- [ ] **AT-03** Keeps phonetic value, rune name, literal name meaning, poem imagery, ritual use, and modern interpretation distinct.
- [ ] **AT-04** Covers Elder Futhark, Younger Futhark, Futhorc, and medieval Scandinavian runes.
- [ ] **AT-05** Checks historical bind-runes with actual inscription signa and more than one period.
- [ ] **AT-06** Does not infer magical purpose from ligature shape alone.

**Comparative verdict: ADAPT, not clone.** Runor and RuneS-DB address object discovery, chronology and corpus context. Menota and EpiDoc address representation and scholarly editorial quality. IIIF and W3C Web Annotation address exact visual/source anchors. Rundata supplies a documented reuse path with special terms. A credible Rune Binding research workbench would connect these functions without pretending that archival catalogues provide verified ancient magical meanings.

**Strong counterevidence to a naive design:** A beautiful modern rune generator could be useful to practitioners, yet it cannot validate ancient provenance. Conversely a rigorous catalogue might have no usable contemporary visual-composition UI. Therefore historical-source authority and user creativity need structurally independent data tracks.

**Accessibility limits of this review:** Direct Handrit manuscript entry fetches and the Danish rune portal fetch were blocked or inaccessible; their data and folios were not verified in this pass. RuneS-DB describes itself as beta. IIIF API availability for any particular archive was not tested. Do not infer actual integration readiness from this comparison.

# 12. New Feature Register - 36 Distinct and Testable Additions


Legend: P0 = indispensable evidence safeguard; P1 = high-value research accelerator; P2 = later expansion. Effort S/M/L = estimated engineering complexity, not elapsed time. All features are **SPECIFIED, NOT IMPLEMENTED**.


## I. Federated sources


### F-01 | Source adapter contract [P0; effort M]


- **New capability:** Use several runic catalogues without pretending their fields or update cadence are identical.

- **Mechanism:** Specify a read-only import interface with source ID, retrieval method, field mapping, license, availability, pagination, format, last checked timestamp, and explicit unsupported fields.

- **Acceptance evidence:** Two differently structured sample source records normalize into one schema with unmapped values preserved and a documented access failure state.

- **Failure boundary:** Do not claim a public API without checking; use manual import or stable deep links where extraction is restricted.

- **Comparative inspiration or method:** Runor / RuneS-DB / Rundata


### F-02 | Cross-corpus identity resolver [P0; effort M]


- **New capability:** Avoid double-counting one object under several signa and catalogues.

- **Mechanism:** Create an alias table joining external signa to one internal witness ID only when repository identity, object description, and cited cross-reference support the match; preserve near-matches unmerged.

- **Acceptance evidence:** A repeated monument merges as one witness with several external aliases, while two similarly named monuments remain distinct.

- **Failure boundary:** False merges are expensive; uncertain links default to suggested rather than automatic.

- **Comparative inspiration or method:** Runor / RuneS-DB


### F-03 | Coverage and denominator explorer [P1; effort M]


- **New capability:** Make claims like most, rare, or common defensible within a defined corpus.

- **Mechanism:** Store query filters, version, geographical/temporal boundaries, total in-scope records, exclusions, missing metadata, and the subset exhibiting the tested property.

- **Acceptance evidence:** Every reported frequency shows numerator, denominator, corpus snapshot, filtering method, and missing-data caveat.

- **Failure boundary:** Different archives have unequal coverage; never silently add raw totals.

- **Comparative inspiration or method:** RuneS-DB / Runor


### F-04 | Source snapshot and drift tracker [P1; effort M]


- **New capability:** Know when a catalogue entry or scholarly reading has changed since the last investigation.

- **Mechanism:** Capture authorized source snapshots, hashes and retrieval dates; compare material fields such as reading, date range, provenance, and external ID between versions.

- **Acceptance evidence:** A changed published interpretation produces an inspectable diff without overwriting the older cited state.

- **Failure boundary:** Respect access terms and retention requirements; content must not be scraped without authorization.

- **Comparative inspiration or method:** Rundata version history / EpiDoc


### F-05 | Rights-aware media gateway [P0; effort S]


- **New capability:** Prevent accidental republication of restricted facsimiles, photos, and database contents.

- **Mechanism:** Assign independently checked permissions for metadata, thumbnails, originals, derivative crops, caching, redistribution, and required credit strings.

- **Acceptance evidence:** An unlicensed image can be viewed through an allowed deep link but cannot appear in a downloadable public evidence pack.

- **Failure boundary:** A public page is not equivalent to an unrestricted reuse license.

- **Comparative inspiration or method:** Rundata reuse conditions / IIIF rights metadata


### F-06 | Geography and find-context explorer [P2; effort M]


- **New capability:** See how inscriptions relate to findspots, current repositories, writing contexts, and movement of objects.

- **Mechanism:** Maintain separate coordinates for archaeological findspot and current holding location, with dated uncertainty radii and no coordinates inferred from vague region labels.

- **Acceptance evidence:** A museum relocation does not change findspot, and a vague regional attribution is rendered as uncertain rather than a precise map pin.

- **Failure boundary:** Geographical gaps and modern repository locations can mislead unless shown separately.

- **Comparative inspiration or method:** Runor map / RuneS-DB


## II. Visual forensics


### F-07 | Region-precise evidence anchors [P0; effort M]


- **New capability:** Let a reader inspect the exact strokes behind a conclusion, not merely a linked page.

- **Mechanism:** Store image ID, IIIF canvas when available, stable URL, normalized xywh polygon, scaling transformation, and citation of the evidence passage; use W3C Web Annotation for export.

- **Acceptance evidence:** Clicking a claim highlights the same region on the original source image after zoom and resize, with a reliable fallback source link.

- **Failure boundary:** Source image must be actually available; never synthesize missing pixels.

- **Comparative inspiration or method:** IIIF / W3C Web Annotation


### F-08 | Folio and page-order integrity checker [P0; effort S]


- **New capability:** Stop manuscript dates and folio/page confusion from contaminating magical-stave attribution.

- **Mechanism:** Represent recto, verso, manuscript foliation, modern printed page, viewer canvas index and any rebinding ambiguity as separate attributes; compare references before publishing.

- **Acceptance evidence:** A mismatch between folio 26v and page 26 triggers a review warning instead of an automatic substitution.

- **Failure boundary:** External descriptions may disagree; unresolved page indexing must stay flagged.

- **Comparative inspiration or method:** IIIF manifests / manuscript catalogues


### F-09 | Stroke-topology signature [P2; effort L]


- **New capability:** Compare two glyphs in terms of geometry rather than evocative resemblance.

- **Mechanism:** Describe endpoints, intersections, branches, connectivity, order constraints and orientation as a graph; save analysis independently of any proposed rune name.

- **Acceptance evidence:** Mirrored, rotated and branch-added test variants produce explainable geometric differences without being labeled historical descendants.

- **Failure boundary:** Damage and artistic copying make a topology score advisory, not authoritative.

- **Comparative inspiration or method:** Digital graphematics in RuneS


### F-10 | Reference-to-reference comparator [P1; effort M]


- **New capability:** Inspect a manuscript image and a contemporary symbol beside one another with precise alignment.

- **Mechanism:** Implement synchronized zoom, transparency overlay, independent rotation, left/right flip flags, and exportable stroke-level discrepancy annotations.

- **Acceptance evidence:** A comparison report lists strokes gained, lost, mirrored, shifted or simplified and links each image to its distinct source.

- **Failure boundary:** Do not conceal transformations: overlays can create deceptive matches.

- **Comparative inspiration or method:** IIIF image region transforms


### F-11 | Similarity candidate queue [P2; effort M]


- **New capability:** Reduce time spent manually searching likely visual relatives without manufacturing lineage.

- **Mechanism:** Offer stroke-level candidate retrieval ranked with disclosed similarity features and source dates; all hits start as GRAPHIC RESEMBLANCE ONLY.

- **Acceptance evidence:** A visually close result cannot become a transmission edge without new external documentary evidence.

- **Failure boundary:** False positives increase with simple radial shapes; human adjudication is mandatory.

- **Comparative inspiration or method:** RuneS graphemics / image annotation


### F-12 | Illegibility and damage layer [P1; effort S]


- **New capability:** Avoid converting missing, worn, cropped or indecipherable marks into confidently decoded runes.

- **Mechanism:** Mark obscured source areas with a region mask; distinguish not visible, damaged, ambiguous stroke and post-processing artifact; attach readings only to visible or explicitly reconstructed geometry.

- **Acceptance evidence:** A deliberately erased branch stays UNKNOWN after auto-analysis and a reconstruction is visually distinguished from the witness.

- **Failure boundary:** Automatic enhancement must never replace the untouched original in the evidence chain.

- **Comparative inspiration or method:** EpiDoc editorial conventions


## III. Philology and semantics


### F-13 | Script and period consistency linter [P0; effort M]


- **New capability:** Catch hybrid Elder/Younger Futhark glyph combinations passed off as one historical inscription.

- **Mechanism:** Check each proposed rune form against the chosen inscription system, chronological range and orthographic context, with documented exceptions and ambiguity handling.

- **Acceptance evidence:** A proposed mixed-alphabet Viking-Age rune name monogram is flagged with the precise incompatible components; a modern mix is allowed when honestly labeled.

- **Failure boundary:** Historical rune variants overlap; linter warnings are not categorical dating proof.

- **Comparative inspiration or method:** Menota / Unicode Runic


### F-14 | Round-trip transliteration checker [P0; effort M]


- **New capability:** Detect information loss when converting glyphs to Latin script, normalized words and translations.

- **Mechanism:** Persist diplomatic glyph sequence, transliteration, normalized text and translation separately; round-trip comparison reports irreversible stages, multi-valued mappings and editorial additions.

- **Acceptance evidence:** Ambiguous phoneme-to-rune reconstruction explicitly lists multiple possibilities rather than claiming a single authentic spelling.

- **Failure boundary:** Exact inverse transliteration is often impossible; mark irreversibility instead of inventing certainty.

- **Comparative inspiration or method:** Menota ch. 18


### F-15 | Alternative reading lattice [P1; effort M]


- **New capability:** Keep multiple scholarly readings alive as structured alternatives rather than averaging them.

- **Mechanism:** Store competing segmentation, direction, rune readings and translated phrases as linked branches with explicit supporting authorities and unresolved branches.

- **Acceptance evidence:** Two defensible alternatives remain independently citable in exported analysis and downstream conclusions inherit their uncertainty.

- **Failure boundary:** Readers may disagree for legitimate reasons; majority vote is not a substitute for philology.

- **Comparative inspiration or method:** EpiDoc / Menota


### F-16 | Ligature boundary analyser [P1; effort M]


- **New capability:** Distinguish real ligatures and same-stave writing from a decorative overlay.

- **Mechanism:** Encode shared strokes and component ordering, including cross-word ligatures, separately from semantic keyword overlays and arbitrary contemporary strokes.

- **Acceptance evidence:** A case in which a historical bind-rune crosses a word boundary is represented without forcing one lexical word.

- **Failure boundary:** Requires checked transcription; many shapes allow several structural decompositions.

- **Comparative inspiration or method:** Menota bind-rune encoding


### F-17 | Semantic category firewall [P0; effort S]


- **New capability:** Prevent an interpretation from moving unnoticed across linguistic and historical categories.

- **Mechanism:** Require tagged statements for sound value, rune name, literal etymology, poem imagery, ritual evidence, and modern esoteric association; reject cross-category export without an explicit inference edge.

- **Acceptance evidence:** A modern Fehu prosperity keyword cannot appear as a documented Viking-Age spell effect without a separate supporting witness.

- **Failure boundary:** Cannot decide scholarly debates alone; enforces claim types and needed evidence.

- **Comparative inspiration or method:** EpiDoc editorial separation


### F-18 | Etymology and language register [P1; effort M]


- **New capability:** Stop an English modern keyword being treated as an Old Norse source term.

- **Mechanism:** Attach reconstructed protolanguage forms, attested medieval words, manuscript spellings and modern translation choices as distinct dated and sourced lexemes.

- **Acceptance evidence:** A contested reconstruction is displayed with its argument and period, not silently turned into an attested inscription.

- **Failure boundary:** Critical dictionaries and manuscripts still need human checking.

- **Comparative inspiration or method:** Menota / philological dictionaries


## IV. Claims and historical transmission


### F-19 | Claim-dependency change propagation [P0; effort M]


- **New capability:** Make later conclusions automatically aware when a decisive source or date is revised.

- **Mechanism:** Connect assertions to constituent source, witness, date, transcription and interpretation records with typed support edges; invalidate only dependent derived claims after edits.

- **Acceptance evidence:** Changing the date of a cited manuscript marks each affected origin claim NEEDS RECHECK without modifying unrelated claims.

- **Failure boundary:** Avoid cycles and accidental propagation from resemblance-only edges.

- **Comparative inspiration or method:** Evidence graph architecture


### F-20 | Chronological contradiction detector [P0; effort S]


- **New capability:** Catch impossible ancestry and retrojected modern labels.

- **Mechanism:** Check earliest/latest witness ranges, uncertain dates, copy dates, proposed origin dates and transmission directions; flag, rather than silently resolve, impossible or unproven sequences.

- **Acceptance evidence:** A twentieth-century design cannot be used as positive proof of a ninth-century Viking glyph merely because its component runes are old.

- **Failure boundary:** Survival bias prevents ruling out undocumented predecessors solely from an early known date.

- **Comparative inspiration or method:** Manuscript dating practice


### F-21 | Independent-source clusterer [P0; effort M]


- **New capability:** Stop ten websites repeating one unsourced symbol chart from posing as ten corroborations.

- **Mechanism:** Track shared images, edition references, bibliographies, author attribution, copied text and dates; group derivative pages under one provisional evidence lineage.

- **Acceptance evidence:** Five near-identical modern chart copies count as one upstream claim family unless independent witnesses are demonstrated.

- **Failure boundary:** Similar wording does not always prove direct copying; clusters need review.

- **Comparative inspiration or method:** Source criticism / scholarly editions


### F-22 | Disagreement dossier [P1; effort S]


- **New capability:** Show genuine scholarly disagreement without false consensus.

- **Mechanism:** Store the exact disputed proposition, positions, cited source passages, different methods, and unresolved access; render parallel arguments rather than a combined averaged verdict.

- **Acceptance evidence:** A debated ritual reading displays strongest arguments for both interpretations and what evidence could discriminate between them.

- **Failure boundary:** Not all positions carry equal evidence; disagreement is not proof of equivalence.

- **Comparative inspiration or method:** Scholarly editorial apparatus


### F-23 | Transmission-edge burden-of-proof gate [P0; effort S]


- **New capability:** Block visual similarity being recast as documented continuity.

- **Mechanism:** Constrain edges to DOCUMENTED COPYING, STRONG ATTRIBUTION, POSSIBLE INFLUENCE or GRAPHIC RESEMBLANCE, each with a source-level threshold and independent audit trail.

- **Acceptance evidence:** Only a cited chain of transmission can earn DOCUMENTED COPYING; unsourced similarity stays resemblance even if nearly identical.

- **Failure boundary:** Traceability is evidence for claims, not certainty about unknowable author intent.

- **Comparative inspiration or method:** Historical stemmatics


### F-24 | Multiple firsts timeline [P1; effort M]


- **New capability:** Explain why earliest symbol, first name and first claimed magical purpose may fall in different centuries.

- **Mechanism:** Store first located form, name, function, print publication, modern reuse and first observed online circulation separately with confidence and location.

- **Acceptance evidence:** An early literary name does not inherit the copying date of a later manuscript illustration, and vice versa.

- **Failure boundary:** First located is never declared first ever without a justified exhaustive evidence basis.

- **Comparative inspiration or method:** Manuscript source chronology

## Minimal portable storage model

Prefer a small **SQLite metadata database plus a content-addressed local file store** if implementation is approved. Keep the design machine-independent: UTF-8 Markdown and JSON/CSV exports are mandatory; external providers are optional. No mandatory network service or account login is needed for working with a saved, authorized research pack.

Recommended tables/collections:

- `source`: source ID, author/publisher, URL, access/license, normalized citation, retrieval time, source-content digest.
- `witness`: internal ID, signa aliases, object/manuscript identity, date range, material, geography and repositories.
- `source_snapshot`: raw allowed bytes reference, digest, version, retrieval and reuse restrictions.
- `region_annotation`: witness/image ID, original coordinates, selector type, transformation and edition state.
- `reading_hypothesis`: script, direction, component geometry, diplomatic, transliteration, normalization and translation alternatives.
- `claim`: atomic proposition, typed claim dimension, status, provenance, confidence rationale, subject and date scope.
- `claim_evidence`: claim ID, witness ID, exact region/folio/passage, support/refute relation and independent-source cluster.
- `design`: vector strokes and historical or modern domain label.
- `transmission_edge`: typed source -> target relation plus cited evidence and chronology.
- `search_receipt`: corpus version, query, limitations, negative results, searched and skipped sources.
- `checkpoint`: idempotent phase/task ID, completed/blocked/pending, dependency digests and replay metadata.
- `design_receipt`: modern creative decisions, authorship, date, transformations and default-private fields.

**Technical invariants:** source witness identity is not a claim of intent; source records are never overwritten destructively; an unresolved alternative may not be silently dropped; derivative modern art is never promoted to manuscript evidence; all outputs name their source and version.

## Two distinct authority channels

1. **Historical evidence lane:** Sources constrain what may be asserted; glyphs, dates, readings and uses must be witnessed or qualified.
2. **Contemporary creative lane:** The maker decides the meaning and artistic form; the resulting provenance receipt states that it is modern and never claims that an uncovered historical original exists.

This separation is intentional, not a hierarchy judging whether a modern practice has personal significance.

# 14. Golden User Journey: One Screenshot to an Audit-Ready Case

**Example workflow, not a claim about an actual uploaded screenshot:**

1. User supplies a screenshot labeled "Viking binding for protection". The system stores the exact original pixels and an image ID.
2. Each visible symbol is boxed or traced and gets its own item ID; unreadable regions receive explicit uncertainty masks.
3. Script compatibility checks inspect the claimed component runes, noting mixed-era possibilities without assigning a definitive origin.
4. A geometry search suggests two lookalikes, initially classified GRAPHIC RESEMBLANCE ONLY.
5. A corpus search checks inscriptions and manuscript inventories by exact signum, description, alternate name and earliest located dated witness.
6. A documented later witness, if any, is linked by exact folio or image region; a web page merely using the same label is marked modern circulation evidence.
7. The claim graph tests "was used for protection" separately from "is a historical runic graphic" and "appears in the Viking Age".
8. A reader can switch between historical form, scholarly interpretation and modern creative use without merging the three.
9. The casefile exports an exact statement of what was found, what was not found, where searching stopped, and every source URL, page/folio and rights restriction.
10. A new contradictory source updates the linked claims and triggers review, rather than silently rewriting previous conclusions.

**Pass:** The case can be independently followed back to its actual source witness with the same verdict constraints.
**Block:** Source images, relevant manuscript pages, or necessary bibliographic witness data are missing or restricted.

## Runtime economy safeguards

- Keep source acquisition and semantic interpretation decoupled; cache only source data whose terms permit it.
- Deduplicate by underlying physical witness, not title string or website count.
- Query primary sources first for high-impact origin claims; stop duplicate web results when saturation is demonstrated.
- Process image regions on demand; never OCR enormous manuscripts by default.
- Use staged metadata before imagery; keep expensive image matching as optional, human-reviewed retrieval.
- Batch source citations per claim, but preserve exact folio/page and region links.
- Cache immutable allowed snapshots by content hash; invalidate only affected derived records.
- Persist useful progress after every bounded batch and validate checkpoint replay after interruptions.
- Honor rate limits, robots, licensing and access controls; no scraping bypass or access impersonation.
- Count unique independently sourced claims, not just search result volume, as useful research throughput.

# 19. Final Editorial Standard (inherited from v2)

For each attractive claim, ask:

> **Where is the earliest datable object, inscription, manuscript witness, book page, or archived image that shows this exact feature? What does that witness *actually* support?**

For every arrow linking one rune-binding tradition to another, ask:

> **What proves transmission, not just visual similarity or plausible influence?**

For every claim that cannot be proven, ask:

> **What did we actually search, what remains inaccessible, and what weaker statement can we defend?**

The outcome must enable the reader to inspect almost any online "ancient bind rune," separate its **graphic identity**, **date**, **linguistic content**, **ritual attribution**, and **documented transmission**, and understand how to make modern runic art with historical honesty.

**Do not start writing the final verdict before the evidence exists. Do not make the investigation seem complete by hiding unresolved records. Accuracy is the feature.**
